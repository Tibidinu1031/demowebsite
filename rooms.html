Fernhollow / Rooms & fees

Three rooms, and a door between them

Forty-one children in total. Children move up when they are ready rather than in September, and most spend a term drifting between two rooms before anybody makes it official.

Room 01

The Nest

Ages 2½ – 3

Twelve children, three adults, and a room that never really gets loud. Most of the day happens on the floor — there are four chairs in the Nest and they are all at the snack table.

We are relaxed about diapers and unrelaxed about the nap. Children arrive here barely talking and leave able to put on their own boots, which they will tell you about at length.

A morning in the Nest

  • Slow arrivals until 9:00. Nobody is greeted loudly and nobody is rushed off a parent.
  • Heuristic play with real objects — tins, brushes, chains, pinecones. Very little plastic.
  • Out to the small garden by 10:15, whatever the sky is doing.
  • Lunch at 11:45, half an hour before the big room, so it stays calm.
  • Sleep from 12:30. Priya sits in the room for the whole of it.

Room 02

The Grove

Ages 3 – 5

Kindergarten proper, and the year the questions get big. The morning block runs ninety minutes without interruption: clay, water, construction, letters, bookmaking, and whatever the current obsession is. In spring 2026 it was pulleys.

Children leave us reading, mostly. More importantly they leave us persistent — able to stay with something that is not working yet. That is the thing their first-grade teachers write to us about.

What’s different here

  • Tomás keeps the woodwork bench open three mornings a week. Real saws, real vices, one adult per two children.
  • Every child cooks with Ana at least once a fortnight, in a group of four.
  • Forest Friday: the whole room walks to Alder Wood at 9:30 and comes back at 2:00.
  • No worksheets. Writing happens because there is a sign to make or a letter to send.

Room 03

Hollow Club

Ages 5 – 11

After school and all summer. We walk to Marlow Elementary, Beckett Road and St. Aldate’s and bring everyone back here by 3:40. There is a proper hot snack, because school lunch was four hours ago and nobody can think.

Then the shape of the afternoon is: something with your hands, something with other people, and then an hour of nothing much. The last part is the one parents ask about and the one the children protect.

Clubs, chosen each term

Woodwork Allotment Choir Chess Bike repair Zine club Cooking

Homework gets an hour at the long table from 5:00, with an adult who will sit with you but will not do it for you. Hollis reads aloud from 5:40 — badly, on purpose, because it makes the older ones correct him and that means they are listening.

What the fee includes

Breakfast, lunch and two snacks, all cooked here. Waterproofs and boots in every size. Every trip we can walk to. All materials, including the wood and the clay. And the Wednesday loaf, which goes home with two children a week, drawn by lottery.

What it doesn’t

Diapers and wipes. The Grove leavers’ overnight in June, which is $40. And the kiln fee if you want to keep the pot, which is $6 and which we will waive if you ask us to.

Siblings and support

Second child is 10% off; with a third we stop counting. We hold four part-funded places each year and the process for those is a conversation with Marguerite, not a form.

Notice and deposits

One calendar month’s notice either way. A two-week deposit holds a place and is credited against your final invoice — it is not a fee, and you get it back.

Availability

Where the list stands today

We publish this honestly and update it the day it changes. We do not hold places back for anybody, and the list is in date order with one exception: siblings of current children go first.
